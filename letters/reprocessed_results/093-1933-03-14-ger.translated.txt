My dear little Bremen, March 14, 1933! Ellen is gone again, and I have to deal with the stupid customers all by myself. It was very nice in Hamburg, even if a bit strange. I&#39;ll tell you personally why, my love. She was, as always, very sweet to me, almost too sweet. As good as that feels, it still creates an obligation, perhaps rightly so, and that&#39;s the only thing that always bothers me! Not that Ellen would demand anything from me, no, but she&#39;s far too much of a woman for a certain desire not to be lurking in her subconscious. Breaking up with Ellen right now would certainly be good (for her). But I would find it unfair, and she certainly doesn&#39;t deserve to be unfair to her. No need to worry about me, my love, because of the political situation. Perhaps I wrote to you about it in a somewhat agitated way due to the general depression. For me, there was absolutely no danger, I was so aware of the general situation, and in the meantime everything has become completely calm again, and the expected revolution has subsided. For the time being, there will be a bit of Jewish bashing, but that too will stop in a few weeks. Objectively, I&#39;m hardly suffering at all; the only thing one can do is wait and seemingly resign oneself to everything. Unfortunately, in Germany today, politics is conducted with pipe boots (high boots) and parade marches. The German spirit has either been lost in political matters or never had it in the first place. Walter is ill and is, of course, suffering particularly because of it. Despite everything, there is a rather optimistic mood among the population for the future, and business is more good than bad. However, there are very bad reports from abroad. Due to the autarky practiced in Germany (= focusing the economy solely on Germany, without regard for other countries), other countries have almost completely closed themselves off to German goods. I hope your dear mother is feeling much better. Was she really so upset? Please give her my regards and send my best wishes for a speedy recovery. And Easter! May nothing get in the way. I have very little money, but one thing I know for sure: either I come to Paris, or you come to Pforzheim. Your mother even wrote to me today saying she&#39;d love to have you with her over Easter. I&#39;d much rather meet you in some small town. But if, in the worst-case scenario, that&#39;s not possible, then we&#39;ll have to make do. If the trip becomes too expensive for you, my love, carry as much as you need. We absolutely must see each other! I&#39;m going to Hanover on Monday and think I&#39;ll be home in three weeks, around April 5th. I&#39;ll be in Hanover until March 25th, with my aunt/Karpfen Rückerst. I&#39;d like to ask you again, my love, not to worry about the political situation. It&#39;s all sad, though, because of the socialist henchmen, but it&#39;s no longer dangerous at all. But that&#39;s all irrelevant if we can happily see each other again at Easter. I love you so much. Yours truly, Maximilian Vergenthal, Pforzheim, 20 98 MEN 15 3 13 23-24 25 Fri.
