Liginian, July 2, 1940 My Everything, my darling love, I have finally received news from you, which, after your letter from Périgueux, reassured me about your fate. I was afraid you were being taken on board in appalling conditions and sailing to who knows what country. You are heading down towards the Pyrenees. You will be reunited with your comrades. Let us hope that the future holds a humanly reasonable life for us; that is all I ask. I wrote a long letter to your postal district, another to Périgueux, but I certainly received nothing from you. I have so much to tell you that I won&#39;t do anything at all in this letter. Especially since all I have to do is give you the address in Narbonne. Here, in a nutshell, is what is happening to us; we are still in Liginian. We intend to stay here for a while longer. Perhaps Betty and Robert will go to Paris to see what has become of our belongings. In any case, until the end of the month. We spend very little money (450 francs per person) and live as well as possible. I am in good health, but not hearing from you is truly the most terrible thing that could happen to me. I&#39;ve only known for three weeks now. I don&#39;t want to delude myself anymore. I&#39;m waiting for you to tell me what you want to do, whether you want me to go where you are or if you want to come here. I can&#39;t work or ask for anything. I don&#39;t know if the Hérault region isn&#39;t better for us, if we&#39;re allowed to stay there. I thought it might be good to try working as a postman in Béziers or elsewhere. Robert, you mean everything to me. I love you more than words can express, but your little thoughts matter. Mr. Robert Zentall, Poste Restante, Béziers (Hérault), Liginiac (Corrèze)
